# The scan at the star's own position

`Scan.from_position` takes the scan of the nearest of the 49,152 sky nodes of gaiamock, about 0.9 degrees apart. On the accuracy scoreboard, moving a star by 0.5 degree to another node changes its predicted parallax and proper-motion offsets by 1.0 to 1.1 predicted errors and its log RUWE by 0.036. This directory holds the scan of the star's exact position and the comparison with the nearest node.

## Sources

1. ESA's Gaia Observation Forecast Tool (GOST, https://gaia.esac.esa.int/gost/). The 49,152 node tables of gaiamock have GOST's own columns (Target, ra, dec, CcdRow, scanAngle, parallaxFactorAlongScan, ObservationTimeAtBarycentre), so each node is a GOST run at the centre of a HEALPix pixel. At the position of node 0 GOST returns the node's 46 transits in the DR3 window; the times differ by a median of 5.8 s and at most 10.7 s, the scan angles by at most 6.4e-6 rad and the along-scan parallax factors by at most 4.2e-6 (tests/test_gost_scan.py, read on 2026-11-24, GOST 22.4.3). `validation/gost_fetch.py` fetches batches of positions as the web form does, and `Scan.from_gost` and `Scan.from_gost_batches` read them.
2. Gaia's commanded scanning law, a continuous table of the attitude every 10 s between 2014-07-25 10:31:26 and 2017-05-28 08:46:29, 8,967,691 rows (the gaiadr3 table commanded_scan_law, 1.1 GB in the archive). The copy outside the archive, in Gaia's bulk-download storage, is one file of the EDR3 table, Gaia/gedr3/auxiliary/commanded_scan_law/CommandedScanLaw_001.csv.gz (569,780,480 bytes, https://cdn.gea.esac.esa.int/Gaia/gedr3/auxiliary/commanded_scan_law/, with its MD5 sum, citation and disclaimer beside it and the licence at Gaia/gedr3/_license.txt). Its text is the same as that of the gaiadr3 table, and it covers 2014-07-25 to 2017-05-28. A scan can be computed from it locally at any position with no web service. This is the second step, tested against GOST at the test positions; it has not been built.
3. The scanninglaw package (Boubert and Everall, https://github.com/gaiaverse/scanninglaw) is under GPL-2.0, which wobbler's MIT licence does not take in, and its laws are maps on a sky grid; it is not used.

## What GOST is

The page says that GOST is a forecast based on the scanning operations, that it does not model operational activities or the gaps between CCDs, that the probability of the data being received at the indicated time is about 80 percent, and that the predicted observation time may deviate by up to 6 hours from the actual one. The nodes of gaiamock carry the same caveat, so the exact position removes the error of the position and not this one.

## Question and rules, written before any scoreboard run

Question: is the position of the scan a main cause of the width of the offset residual of the scoreboard (z of 2.4 to 2.7 for the three offsets) and of the width of the RUWE ratio?
Hypothesis, argued: a star is on average a fraction of a degree from its node, which changes which transits it has and their scan angles, so the exact scan lowers both widths. What would show it wrong: no width falls by more than its bootstrap error.
Run: the 60,940 stars of G below 13 of the scoreboard sample, the scan of each from GOST at its Gaia DR3 position (the target is its source id), the same 100 draws, seeds, B(G) refit on half A by the scoreboard's own code, and the comparison with the nearest-node run by the scoreboard's improvement rule: a quantity improves when its overall width falls by more than the paired-bootstrap standard error of the difference and in no bin used in the comparison does the width rise by more than the standard error of the difference there. Decision: the exact scan is kept as an option and stays off by default until the scoreboard decides.
The positions are those of gaia_source (epoch 2016.0, ICRS); the scan of a star that moves by its proper motion over the mission is that of its 2016.0 position (argued: the motion over 34 months is under 0.1 arcsec for all but a few stars, against the 0.9 degrees of the nodes).

Amendment 1 (2026-11-25, before the run and before any scoreboard number of the exact scans).
Check of the scans before the run: every one of the 60,940 positions of G below 13 has rows in the exported batches, no target is a star outside the sample, no row is listed twice (the same target and barycentric time), and the transits in the DR3 window number at least 6 for every star (the fit needs 6 epochs). A star without a scan, or a duplicated row, is reported and the run does not start until it is read.
Statistics compared with the nearest-node run, on the 30,470 stars of half B of G below 13: the median and width of x = ln(RUWE archive / RUWE predicted), of the residual z and of the pull of the parallax, pmra and pmdec, and of the logarithm of the ratio of the formal errors, overall and in the bins of the scoreboard. B(G) is refit on half A of the exact-scan simulation by the scoreboard's own code (as the Run paragraph says), and the nearest-node baseline is its own scored run with its own half-A fit; the comparison is of two complete runs. The improvement rule is that of the scoreboard (`validation/scoreboard.py compare`): a statistic improves when its overall width falls by more than the paired-bootstrap error and no bin used rises by more than its error.
What the hypothesis predicts, argued: with the scan mismatch removed, the width of the pull falls toward the 1.64, 1.57 and 1.77 that adding the node shift in quadrature gave (the elements' covariance is not drawn here), and the width of the RUWE ratio falls from 0.098 toward the width of the log RUWE change under a 0.5 degree shift, 0.036 in quadrature (0.098 to 0.091). A pull width that stays above 2.4 and a RUWE width within 0.003 of 0.098 would show that the position of the scan is not a main cause.
The exact scan is GOST's, and GOST's own times carry the difference of 5.8 s median between its versions at a node (Sources, 1), which is far below what the scan angle needs, so it is not a candidate for a residual.

Amendment 2 (2026-11-25, after the check of the scans and before the run). The check found 60,940 distinct targets, none without a scan, none in two files, at least 18 transits in the DR3 window for every star (median 52, 99th percentile 110, maximum 170), and 428 rows that repeat the target and the barycentric time of another row, in 119 stars. The repeats are in GOST's answers, and the nearest node of gaiamock at the position of one such star has them too (75 rows, 67 distinct barycentric times), so the two runs carry them alike and the rows are kept as GOST gives them.

## Result

The simulation of the 60,940 stars of G below 13 on their exact GOST scans took 567 s on the GPU (9.3 ms a star; the estimate was 400 s) with a peak resident memory of 2.6 GB, and none was dropped. The scoreboard was scored on the 30,470 stars of half B with B refit on half A of the run (`scoreboard.json`, `scoreboard_swapped.json` with the halves exchanged), and compared with the nearest-node run by the rule of Amendment 1 (`compare.json`, `validation/scoreboard.py compare`). `check_scans.json` is the check of the scans.

Every compared statistic improves by the rule: its overall width falls by more than its error and no bin used rises by more than its error. The width of the RUWE ratio falls from 0.0979 to 0.0819 (change -0.0160, error 0.0006). The width of the residual z falls from 2.70, 2.41 and 2.64 to 1.83, 1.66 and 1.97 for the parallax, pmra and pmdec, and the width of the pull from 2.60, 2.57 and 2.82 to 1.78, 1.79 and 2.12; the errors of these changes are 0.015 to 0.019. The width of the ratio of the formal errors falls from 0.118, 0.138 and 0.141 to 0.084, 0.099 and 0.102. The medians move by at most 0.03 in the overall statistics. The widths fall in every bin of G, of the period over the window, of the ecliptic latitude and of the number of good observations: the RUWE widths at ecliptic latitude 0 to 15 and 15 to 30 degrees, the widest bins, fall from 0.129 and 0.118 to 0.113 and 0.097. The RUWE median moves farther from zero beyond its error in 3 bins, and the medians of the offsets in 0 to 5 bins for each statistic (the "regressions" of the comparison, listed in `compare.json`). With the halves exchanged the RUWE width is 0.0821 and the pull widths are 1.78, 1.79 and 2.11.

The pull widths for the parallax and pmra, 1.78 and 1.79, are inside the range that the hypothesis predicted (1.64 to 1.77, and a little above it), and the pull width for the pmdec, 2.12, is above it. The RUWE width, 0.082, is below the 0.091 that was predicted. Neither prediction that would show the position of the scan not to be a main cause came true (a pull width above 2.4, a RUWE width within 0.003 of 0.098). B(G) of the exact run differs from that of the nearest-node run by 0.6 percent at most in each of the 16 bands of G 9 to 13 (the ratio of the two fits is 0.994 to 1.005), so the calibration is not the source of the gain.

The position of the scan is therefore a main cause of the width of the offset residual and of the RUWE ratio on these stars; it is not the only one, since the pull widths stay above 1 (1.8 to 2.1). The exact scan is GOST's forecast, which the page says may deviate by up to 6 hours from the observation times, so the widths that remain include that deviation (argued, not measured here).
